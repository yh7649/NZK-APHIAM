# KEPCO Fuel-Technology Emission-Factor Validation Notes

This note records a first-pass external plausibility screen for the annual
KEPCO fuel x technology emission-factor handoff tables generated by
`analysis/kepco/kepco_monthly_analysis.R`.

All values below are comparable as `kg/MWh` and `g/kWh`; the numeric value is
the same because both units express grams per kilowatt-hour.

- `results/tables/kepco/annual_handoff/kepco_annual_ef_handoff_by_fuel_technology.csv`
- `results/tables/kepco/annual_handoff/kepco_annual_ef_handoff_by_province_fuel_technology.csv`

- `results/figures/kepco/fuel_technology_year/year=YYYY/`

The handoff tables keep only observed fuel x technology cohorts. They include:

- generation-weighted annual EF;
- annual plant-level EF mean, standard deviation, p10, p25, median, p75, p90,
  min, max, and IQR;
- plant-month EF mean, standard deviation, p10, p25, median, p75, p90, min,
  max, and IQR;
- plant count, plant-month count, valid generation, generation coverage, and
  date coverage.

The main handoff uses the `operational_primary` monthly eligibility rule: it
excludes structurally invalid pollutant-months, pollutant-specific high-EF or
implausible-zero observations, and positive-generation months below a 1%
monthly capacity factor. Recent pollutant level shifts remain review flags in
the primary estimate because they may represent real control or operating
changes. The accompanying long sensitivity tables compare this primary result
with `low_load_inclusive` and `conservative_quality` specifications.

## Literature and Benchmark Sources

### South Korea aggregate air-pollutant benchmark

The CREA/Solutions for Our Climate report *Bridge to Death* cites South Korean
Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy data reporting aggregate air pollution
intensities of `0.171 kg/MWh` for gas-fired power and `0.561 kg/MWh` for
coal-fired power. The report also notes that gas-plant NOx can be elevated
during startup, shutdown, and partial-load operation.

Source:
<https://energyandcleanair.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/SFOC-Air-Quality_Health-Impacts_Gas-Power_.pdf>

### East Asian fossil-power unit benchmarks

Lee et al. (2021), using Taiwan fossil-fuel power units, report the following
2018 values:

- coal, C-II/C-III: PM `0.0415`/`0.0162`, SOx `0.3203`/`0.1442`, and NOx
  `0.3329`/`0.1810 g/kWh`;
- LNG: PM roughly `0.0085` to `0.0202 g/kWh`, SOx roughly `0.00086` to
  `0.00206 g/kWh`, and NOx roughly `0.1783` to `0.2231 g/kWh`; the same study
  reports LNG NOx unit-level range `0.072` to `0.403 g/kWh`;
- oil, 2018: SO2 `1.157`, NOx `0.805`, and PM `0.056 g/kWh`.

Source:
<https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/18/5716>

### Coal standards and China comparison

CREA's international standards comparison reports coal-plant concentration
limits, not generation-based EFs. Still, it is a useful regulatory context:

- existing coal plants: South Korea `286` SO2, `308` NOx, `36` PM mg/m3;
  China all plants `200` SO2, `100` NOx, `30` PM mg/m3; China 2020 ULE `35`
  SO2, `50` NOx, `10` PM mg/m3;
- new coal plants: South Korea `229` SO2, `164` NOx, `18` PM mg/m3; China
  `35` SO2, `50` NOx, `10` PM mg/m3.

Source:
<https://energyandcleanair.org/comparison-of-coal-power-plant-emissions-standards/>

For China, Liu et al. (2015) report pre-ULE coal power generation-based
emission factors. Their preprint table shows a large decline across 2002-2010
but still reports 2010 coal factors near SO2 `2.48`, NOx `2.67`, and PM2.5
`0.27 g/kWh`, far above post-control Korean/Taiwanese values. Liu et al. (2019)
show that China's later ultra-low-emission retrofits reduced NOx, SO2, and PM
EFs by up to one to two orders of magnitude and emphasized that EF variability
across units and time remains important.

Sources:
<https://acp.copernicus.org/preprints/15/18787/2015/acpd-15-18787-2015.pdf>
<https://www.pku-atmos-acm.org/static/pdfs/Liu%20X%202019%20EST_1.pdf>

## Plausibility Screen

### Coal conventional steam

KEPCO conventional coal is:

- 2021: NOx `0.126`, SOx `0.139`, TSP `0.009 kg/MWh`;
- 2025: NOx `0.087`, SOx `0.118`, TSP `0.007 kg/MWh`.

These are lower than the South Korea aggregate coal air-pollution benchmark
(`0.561 kg/MWh` summed air-pollution intensity) and lower than Taiwan's 2018
coal NOx and PM benchmarks, while SOx is near Taiwan's lower-capacity-class
value. This is plausible for heavily controlled large KEPCO units, but the
comparison suggests we should explain that the table is post-audit, pollutant
specific, and KEPCO-subsidiary scoped rather than a national all-coal inventory.

### Coal IGCC

KEPCO coal IGCC is:

- 2021: NOx `0.106`, SOx `0.005`, TSP `0.003 kg/MWh`;
- 2025: NOx `0.110`, SOx `0.011`, TSP `0.001 kg/MWh`.

The very low SOx/TSP values are directionally plausible for an advanced,
single observed technology cohort, but the cohort is small. Treat as plausible
with a sample-size caveat.

### Natural gas combined cycle

KEPCO natural-gas CCGT is:

- 2021: NOx `0.164`, SOx `0.000`, TSP `0.000 kg/MWh`;
- 2025: NOx `0.095 kg/MWh`; SOx and TSP do not meet the 50% generation-
  coverage threshold for the operational-primary handoff.

The available NOx values align well with the Korean gas aggregate benchmark
(`0.171 kg/MWh`) and Taiwan LNG NOx values (`0.072` to `0.403 g/kWh`). The 2021
source reports SOx/TSP at zero for the retained cohort, while the 2025 primary
table leaves those pollutants unavailable rather than presenting a
low-coverage value.

### Natural gas CHP

KEPCO natural-gas CHP has NOx `0.226 kg/MWh` in 2021 and `0.084 kg/MWh` in
2025. SOx/TSP are unavailable in the handoff table for this cohort. The NOx
values are within the Taiwan LNG range, but the pollutant coverage gap should
be kept visible.

### Biomass and bio-oil/diesel

Biomass and bio-oil/diesel values are plausible in magnitude but lack a strong
Korea-specific benchmark in the sources reviewed here. They should be treated
as internal-data estimates pending a fuel-specific literature review.

### Oil

KEPCO conventional oil is:

- 2021: NOx `0.301`, SOx `0.011`, TSP `0.002 kg/MWh`;
- 2025: NOx `0.215`, SOx `0.002`, TSP `0.012 kg/MWh`.

These are lower than Taiwan's oil NOx/SO2 benchmarks and far lower for SOx,
which may reflect lower sulfur fuel, controls, or reporting boundaries.

The 2025 oil internal-combustion cohort is the strongest follow-up flag:
NOx `1.889`, SOx `0.074`, TSP `0.060 kg/MWh`. NOx is above the Taiwan oil
benchmark (`0.805 g/kWh`), while TSP is similar and SOx is much lower. Because
this cohort has one observed plant, keep it in the handoff table but annotate it
as a small-n, high-NOx cohort for review.

## Recommended Handoff Language

Use the generation-weighted EF as the primary scalar for model input, but show
the plant-level and plant-month distribution columns beside it. For MACRO, the
most useful columns are:

- `{pollutant}_ef_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_plant_ef_median_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_plant_ef_p10_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_plant_ef_p90_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_monthly_ef_median_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_monthly_ef_p10_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_monthly_ef_p90_kg_per_mwh`
- `{pollutant}_plant_count`
- `{pollutant}_plant_month_count`
- `{pollutant}_generation_coverage_pct`

This keeps the weighted average available for modeling while making spread,
coverage, and small-sample caveats explicit.
